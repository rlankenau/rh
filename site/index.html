Companion site

Two claimed proofs of the Riemann Hypothesis, one day

On November 20, 2026 two unrelated proofs of the generalized Riemann Hypothesis were released. This site works through both, one step at a time, with labs you can play with: every figure is computed in your browser from the definitions, and the sliders change the actual functions under discussion.

Neither proof has been verified, and nothing here says either is correct. These are a reader's notes: each chapter says what it is based on and how closely that source was read.

How to use it

  • Part I is shared by both proofs. Read it first, in order.
  • Parts II and III take one proof each: a one-page map of the whole argument, then short chapters that take one step each. Chapters marked planned are not written yet.
  • Each chapter ends with things to try in its lab and exercises with worked solutions, and a three-question check of the main ideas. Each part ends with a longer quiz.
  • Pages marked "needs review" have not yet been checked for content by the site's owner.
  • To comment, select any text and choose "Comment on this", or use the link at the foot of a page. Comments are filed as issues on GitHub, so you need a GitHub account.
  • Words with a dotted underline open a definition. There is a full glossary and a table of Greek letters and symbols.
