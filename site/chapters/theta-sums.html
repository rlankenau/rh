Summing over whole numbers

Chapter @@ch:circle-slots@@ summed a function over the powers of \(\gamma\), and the factor \(1 - \gamma^{-s}\) appeared in front of its Mellin transform. Summing over the whole numbers instead puts \(\zeta(s)\) in front. The resulting functions are the θ-functions of the paper.

Based on §1.1.4 of the paper (page 12), read in full. The lab works over the ordinary integers with the trivial character, which is the Riemann zeta case; the paper works over any number field.

θ-functions

The paper uses the construction of chapter @@ch:circle-slots@@ with a different sum, the one at the center of Tate's thesis. Take a function \(f\) on \((0, \infty)\) and sum its values at all whole-number multiples of \(x\):

$$\Theta_f(x) \;=\; \sqrt{x}\,\sum_{n\ge 1} f(nx)$$ The paper calls functions of this form θ-functions. The Mellin transform is $$M(\Theta_f)(s) \;=\; \zeta\!\left(s+\tfrac12\right)\cdot M(f)\!\left(s+\tfrac12\right)$$

Proof: by the rescaling property the transform of \(f(nx)\) is \(n^{-s} \cdot M(f)(s)\), and \(\sum n^{-s} = \zeta(s)\). Multiplying by \(\sqrt{x}\) replaces \(s\) by \(s + \tfrac12\). (The sum converges for \(\operatorname{Re} s \gt \tfrac12\); the identity extends to all \(s\) by analytic continuation when \(\Theta_f\) decays at both ends.) The factor \(\zeta\) here plays the role that \(1 - \gamma^{-s}\) played on the circle.

So the transform of every θ-function is zero at the zeros of \(\zeta\), whatever \(f\) is. Because of the shift by ½, a zero \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\) appears at \(s = \rho - \tfrac12\). If the Riemann Hypothesis holds these points are all on the imaginary axis, at \(s = \pm 14.13i,\ \pm 21.02i,\ \pm 25.01i,\ \dots\)

Exercises

  1. Show that the Mellin transform of \(f(nx)\) is \(n^{-s} \cdot M(f)(s)\).
    Solution

    Substitute \(u = nx\): $$\int_{0}^{\infty} f(nx)\,x^{s}\,\frac{dx}{x} \;=\; \int_{0}^{\infty} f(u)\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)^{s}\frac{du}{u} \;=\; n^{-s}\cdot M(f)(s)$$ The measure \(dx/x\) is unchanged by rescaling.

  2. The first zero of \(\zeta\) is \(\rho \approx \tfrac12 + 14.13i\). Where is the transform of \(\Theta_f\) zero because of it?
    Solution

    At \(s = \rho - \tfrac12 \approx 14.13i\), since \(M(\Theta_f)(s)\) contains the factor \(\zeta(s + \tfrac12)\). That is the first dot in the lab.

  3. Why does \(\Theta_f(x)\) blow up as \(x \to 0\) unless \(\int f\,dx = 0\)?
    Solution

    For small \(x\) the points \(nx\) are closely spaced, and \(x \cdot \sum f(nx)\) is a Riemann sum for \(\int f\). So \(\sum f(nx) \approx \dfrac{1}{x} \int f\), and \(\Theta_f(x) \approx x^{-1/2} \int f\), which is unbounded unless the integral is 0.

Check your understanding

  1. The transform of \(\Theta_f\) vanishes at \(s = \rho - \tfrac12\) for every zero \(\rho\), whichever \(f\) you choose. What does that tell you?

    • That \(f\) was chosen specially
    • The zeros are a property of summing over the whole numbers, not of \(f\)
    • That \(\Theta_f\) is identically 0

    \(M(\Theta_f)(s) = \zeta(s + \tfrac12) \cdot M(f)(s + \tfrac12)\). The factor \(\zeta\) comes from \(\sum n^{-s}\) and is the same for every \(f\).

  2. What is the effect of the factor \(\sqrt{x}\) in the definition of \(\Theta_f\)?

    • It shifts \(s\) by ½, so zeros on the critical line appear at purely imaginary \(s\)
    • It makes the sum converge
    • It removes the zeros

    Multiplying by \(x^{1/2}\) shifts the Mellin variable by ½. The critical line \(\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac12\) becomes the imaginary axis, where the transform is an ordinary Fourier transform in \(\ln x\).

  3. Suppose you summed \(f(nx)\) over the odd \(n\) only. Which factor would appear in front of \(M(f)\)?

    • \(\zeta(s) + 2^{-s}\)
    • \(\zeta(2s)\)
    • \(\zeta(s) \cdot (1 - 2^{-s})\)

    The even \(n\) contribute \(\sum (2m)^{-s} = 2^{-s}\zeta(s)\). Removing them leaves \(\zeta(s)(1 - 2^{-s})\): the Euler product without the factor for the prime 2.
