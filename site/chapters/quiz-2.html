Quiz: Part II

Twelve questions on Masullo's argument, in the order of the chapters.

Choose an answer to see whether it is right and why. Your best score is kept in this browser only.

  1. "A trace computed two ways" refers to which two computations?

    • Adding the eigenvalues, and integrating the kernel along its diagonal
    • Summing over primes, and summing over whole numbers
    • A proof on paper, and a proof in Lean
    • Integrating over t > 1, and over t < 1

    The sum of eigenvalues is the spectral side; the integral of K(x, x) is the geometric side. A trace formula says they are equal.

  2. On the circle (0, ∞) modulo γ, the function x−s is well defined exactly when:

    • s is real
    • s is a zero of ζ
    • γs = 1
    • s = ½

    The function must agree at x and γx, and (γx)−s = γ−sx−s. So s = 2πim/ln γ: the zeros of 1 − γ−s.

  3. For the circle with γ = 3 and a test function φ that is zero outside [1.2, 2.5], the trace of A(φ) is:

    • ln 3 · φ(3)
    • M(φ)(0)
    • It depends on the shape of φ
    • 0

    The geometric side is ln 3 · Σk φ(3k), and no power of 3 lies in [1.2, 2.5]. The eigenvalues are not individually zero, but they add up to zero.

  4. The Mellin transform of f(γt) is:

    • γ−s · M(f)(s)
    • M(f)(s + γ)
    • M(f)(γs)
    • γ · M(f)(s)

    Substitute u = γt; the measure dt/t is unchanged and ts = γ−sus. Rescaling becomes multiplication, which is why the Mellin transform suits this problem.

  5. Summing f(nx) over all whole numbers n multiplies the Mellin transform of f by:

    • 1 − γ−s
    • ζ(s)
    • 1/ζ(s)
    • s

    Each term contributes n−s · M(f)(s), and Σ n−s = ζ(s). So the transform of a θ-function vanishes at the zeros of ζ (shifted by ½).

  6. In the quotient H (smooth functions modulo θ-functions), an element is determined by:

    • Its values at the primes
    • Its integral
    • The values of its transform at the zeros of the L-function
    • Its values at whole numbers

    Adding a θ-function changes the transform by a multiple of the L-function, which leaves the values at the zeros alone. That is "one slot per zero". The full claim rests on the paper's Structure Theorems.

  7. On the slot of a zero ρ, the operator A(φ) multiplies by:

    • φ(ρ)
    • ζ′(ρ)
    • ln ρ
    • M(φ)(ρ − ½)

    On transforms A(φ) is multiplication by M(φ)(s), and the slot of ρ sits at s = ρ − ½. The sum of these numbers is the spectral side.

  8. With strip half-width a = 0.1, which hypothetical zero would still contribute to Masullo's spectral side?

    • Re ρ = 0.55
    • Re ρ = 0.6
    • Re ρ = 0.75
    • All of them

    Only zeros with |Re ρ − ½| < a keep their slot in the completed space. 0.55 is at distance 0.05; 0.6 is exactly on the edge and is lost.

  9. A test function φ is zero outside [2, 20]. The contribution of the prime 23 to the geometric side is:

    • ln 23 · φ(23)/√23, which is small
    • 0
    • ln 23
    • Unknown without computing

    The contribution is ln p · Σ φ(pk)/pk/2, and no power of 23 is in [2, 20]. Only finitely many primes contribute for any one test function.

  10. Masullo's formula differs from the classical explicit formula in:

    • The prime sum
    • The term from the pole of ζ
    • Nothing: they are the same formula
    • Which zeros are summed over

    The classical formula sums over every zero; Masullo's only over those within a of the critical line. The two agree for every a exactly when the hypothesis is true.

  11. In the last step, why does the formula for the test functions φz exclude zeros with Re ρ > ½ + a?

    • Such a zero would be a pole of ζ′/ζ in a region where every other term is holomorphic
    • Such a zero would make the prime sum negative
    • The lab checks all such zeros numerically
    • Because the functional equation forbids them

    Every term other than ζ′/ζ has no pole with Re z > a except z = ½, so ζ′/ζ cannot have one either. The functional equation only mirrors zeros; it does not forbid them.

  12. Which statement about the status of the proof is accurate?

    • The labs on this site confirm it
    • It has been checked in Lean
    • It is unverified; the steps that carry it are the ones this site has not checked
    • It has been refuted

    The labs test the classical arithmetic that the construction reproduces. The completion of the exact sequence and the computation of the geometric side in the completed spaces are the load-bearing steps, and nobody has verified them yet.
