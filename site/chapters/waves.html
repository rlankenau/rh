Zeros as waves

The prime staircase can be rebuilt from the zeros of zeta, one wave per zero. This is the picture behind the title of Masullo's paper, and it is the reason the position of the zeros decides the size of the error.

The explicit formula

Riemann's explicit formula, in the form von Mangoldt proved, is an exact identity between the weighted staircase and the zeros ρ of zeta in the critical strip:

ψ(x) = x − Σρ xρ/ρ − ln 2π − ½ ln(1 − x−2)

The first term is the smooth guess. The last two are tiny. Everything interesting is the sum over zeros. Write a zero as ρ = β + iγ. Together with its mirror image below the axis it contributes a real wave:

  • its pitch is γ: the wave is cos(γ ln x), so it oscillates evenly on a logarithmic scale;
  • its loudness grows like xβ, and is divided by roughly γ, so high zeros are quiet.

What a zero off the line would do

If every zero has β = ½, every wave has loudness about √x, and the total error is about √x: the bound from chapter 1. A single zero with β > ½ would contribute a wave of size xβ, which eventually drowns out all the others. That is the entire content of the equivalence between RH and the square-root error bound.

The mirror symmetry matters here. A zero at β < ½ forces a partner at 1 − β > ½, so any zero off the line produces a too-loud wave.
