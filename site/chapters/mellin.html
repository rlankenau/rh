The Mellin transform

The Mellin transform is the Fourier transform for functions on (0, ∞), with rescaling t ↦ γt in the role of translation. This chapter states the definition and the two properties that the rest of Part II uses.

Standard material. The paper uses the Mellin transform throughout and defines it on page 17.

Definition

For a function f on (0, ∞) and a complex number s,

$$M(f)(s) \;=\; \int_{0}^{\infty} f(t)\, t^{s}\, \frac{dt}{t}$$

The substitution t = eu turns this into ∫ f(eu) esu du over the whole real line. For s = iy that is the Fourier transform of f in the variable u = ln t. If f is zero outside a bounded interval [A, B] with A > 0, the integral converges for every complex s, and M(f) is an entire function.

Property 1: rescaling becomes multiplication

The transform of f(γt) is $$\gamma^{-s}\cdot M(f)(s)$$

Proof: substitute u = γt. The measure dt/t does not change, and ts = γ−sus. Consequently convolution on (0, ∞), which is a weighted sum of rescalings, becomes multiplication of transforms: M(A(φ)f)(s) = M(φ)(s) · M(f)(s) for the operator A(φ) of chapter @@ch:m1-trace-two-ways@@.

Property 2: the real part of s weighs large t against small t

Write s = σ + iy. Then |ts| = tσ. For σ > 0 this factor is large where t > 1 and small where t < 1. So if f ≥ 0 is zero outside [A, B],

$$A^{\sigma}\cdot M(f)(0) \;\le\; M(f)(\sigma) \;\le\; B^{\sigma}\cdot M(f)(0) \qquad \text{for } \sigma\ge 0$$

A function concentrated at t > 1 has a transform that grows as σ increases, and one concentrated at t < 1 has a transform that shrinks.

Things to try

  • Center the bump at t = 1. The two curves nearly coincide for every σ: the factor tσ is close to 1 across the bump.
  • Narrow the bump. Both curves spread out to larger y: a narrow function has a wide transform.
  • Put the bump at t ≈ 3 and compare the readout with 3σ.

Exercises

  1. Let f(t) = 1 for 1 ≤ t ≤ T and 0 otherwise. Compute M(f)(s).
    Solution

    $$\int_{1}^{T} t^{\,s-1}\,dt \;=\; \frac{T^{s}-1}{s} \quad (s\ne 0), \qquad \ln T \quad (s=0)$$

  2. For which s does rescaling by γ = 2 leave the transform unchanged?
    Solution

    The factor is 2−s, which equals 1 when s ln 2 = 2πim, that is, s = 2πim/ln 2 for an integer m. These are the Fourier modes of the circle in chapter @@ch:m1-trace-two-ways@@.

  3. Show that the transform of t · f′(t) is −s · M(f)(s), for smooth f that is zero outside a bounded interval [A, B] with A > 0.
    Solution

    $$\int_{0}^{\infty} t\,f^{\prime}(t)\,t^{s}\,\frac{dt}{t} \;=\; \int_{0}^{\infty} f^{\prime}(t)\,t^{s}\,dt \;=\; -s\int_{0}^{\infty} f(t)\,t^{\,s-1}\,dt \;=\; -s\cdot M(f)(s)$$ The middle step is integration by parts; the boundary terms vanish because f is zero outside [A, B].

Check your understanding

  1. Why is the measure dt/t used in place of dt?

    • It makes every integral converge
    • It is unchanged by rescaling t ↦ γt, which is what makes rescaling turn into multiplication by γ−s
    • It is just a convention with no consequences

    With u = γt, du/u = dt/t. With plain dt an extra factor γ would appear. In the coordinate ln t it is ordinary length, and rescaling is translation.

  2. A function f ≥ 0 is zero outside [0.1, 0.5]. As σ increases from 0, M(f)(σ):

    • shrinks, because tσ < 1 where f lives
    • grows
    • stays the same

    By property 2 the value lies between 0.1σ and 0.5σ times M(f)(0). Functions living at t > 1 behave the opposite way, which matters later because the proof uses test functions supported at t > 1.

  3. You make the bump narrower. Its transform along a vertical line:

    • becomes narrower too
    • is unchanged
    • spreads out to larger heights y

    A function concentrated in a short range of ln t needs high frequencies to describe it. Later this means a narrow test function "sees" zeros at greater heights.
