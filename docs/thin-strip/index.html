Part II · Chapter 10

The thin strip

Masullo completes the spaces of chapter 9 in a that depends on a number a > 0. In the completed space only the zeros with |Re ρ − ½| < a keep their slots. The paper presents this step as new.

Based on §1.1.3 and §1.2.1 of the paper (pages 11–14), read in full. The paper states the norm and which zeros contribute. The explanation below of why those are the zeros that remain is my own reading; the paper's argument is in chapters I have not read.

The norm

Fix a > 0. For a function ϕ on (0, ∞), set

‖ϕ‖a² = 0∞ |ϕ(t)|² · max(t2a, t−2a) dt/t.

In the coordinate u = t the weight is e2a|u|: a function has finite norm when it decays faster than e−a|u| in both directions. This is the paper's Hardy–Sobolev norm with base interval [−a, a] and smoothness index 0, written here for (0, ∞) in place of the paper's group. Completing a space in this norm means adding the limits of all sequences that converge in it. The result is a . The paper says these spaces "were never considered in the literature".

The paper also gives the norm on the transform side: it is equivalent to the size of M(ϕ) along the two vertical lines Re s = a and Re s = −a, the edges of a strip of width 2a.

Which slots remain

The slot at a point z is the number M(ϕ)(z). It remains meaningful on the completed space only if it is controlled by the norm, so that it passes to limits. Let x = Re z.

  • Inside the strip, |x| < a. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, |M(ϕ)(z)| ≤ ‖ϕ‖a · √(a/(a² − x²)). The slot is controlled.
  • On the edge or outside, |x| ≥ a. There are functions ϕT with M(ϕT)(x)/‖ϕT‖a → ∞ as T → ∞. No bound exists, and the slot is lost.

The lab uses ϕT(t) = tx / max(t2a, t−2a) for 1/T ≤ t ≤ T and 0 elsewhere, which is the function that makes the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality an equality.

The spectral side in the completed space

Complete the three spaces of the divisor in this norm. The paper's claim is that the sequence stays exact, that A(φ) acts on the completed as a -class , and that its trace is

N(ρ) · M(φ)(ρ − ½), summed over the zeros with |Re ρ − ½| < a only.

So at distance a or more from the contributes nothing to this trace. The half-width a can be chosen as small as desired. The paper places this between two earlier approaches: in 's work every zero contributes, wherever it is, and 's "spectral interpretation" is described as the limiting case in which the strip is the critical line alone.

Removing terms from one side of an identity is only useful if the other side can still be computed. The classical (chapter 12) is a theorem, and it contains every zero. The proof therefore depends on computing the other side of the trace inside the completed space, without using the classical formula. Chapters 11 and 12 describe how the paper does that.

Things to try

  • Set a = 0.3 and move x from 0.2 up through 0.3. The bound rises without limit as x approaches a, and at x = a the curve no longer levels off.
  • Set x = 0.1, the position of a hypothetical zero at β = 0.6. Find the values of a for which its slot is lost.

Exercises

  1. For the lab's ϕT, show that M(ϕT)(x) = ‖ϕT‖a² and compute it for |x| < a.
    Solution

    Write w(t) for the weight. Both quantities equal IT = ∫ t2x/w(t) dt/t over [1/T, T]. On [1, T] the integrand is t2x − 2a, giving (1 − T−2(a − x))/(2(a − x)). On [1/T, 1] it is t2x + 2a, giving (1 − T−2(a + x))/(2(a + x)). The ratio in the lab is √IT. As T → ∞, IT → 1/(2(a − x)) + 1/(2(a + x)) = a/(a² − x²).

  2. With a = 0.1, which of these hypothetical zeros would keep a slot: β = 0.55, β = 0.6, β = 0.7?
    Solution

    The slot is at β − ½: 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2. Only the first is strictly inside the strip. The zero at β = 0.6 is exactly on the edge and is lost, as is the one at 0.7.

  3. Show that max(t2a, t−2a) = e2a|u| for u = ln t.
    Solution

    t±2a = e±2au. The larger of e2au and e−2au is the one with the positive exponent, e2a|u|.
