  1. Why does the formalization work with a smoothed prime sum?

    • Smoothing removes the zeros
    • After smoothing the amplitudes are about 2/γ22/\gamma^{2}2/γ2, so the sum over all zeros converges absolutely and can be bounded
    • Lean cannot handle step functions

    The total of all amplitudes is about 0.0462 in units of x\sqrt{x}x​. Without smoothing the corresponding sum diverges.

  3. The formalization proves only an upper bound. Why does that exclude zeros off the line?

    • An off-line zero adds an oscillation of size xβx^{\beta}xβ that takes both signs, and by Landau's theorem the other terms cannot cancel it, so it would break an upper bound too
    • Because the error is always negative
    • It does not; a lower bound is still needed

    Lab 20.2 shows it: with β>12\beta \gt \tfrac12β>21​ the curve leaves the band through the top and the bottom.

  4. A Lean development compiles, but one lemma that the main theorem uses is closed with sorry. What has been proved?

    • The main theorem
    • The main theorem on the assumption that the lemma is true
    • Nothing at all

    Lean accepts sorry in place of any proof and records the dependency. Everything else is checked; the result is conditional until the gap is filled. This is the situation of trace_identity in this site's own Lean experiments.

  5. How does this proof's strategy differ in kind from Masullo's?

    • Both use a trace formula
    • This one counts zeros by computer
    • This one bounds a prime sum from one side and converts the bound into a statement about zeros; Masullo's compares the two sides of a trace formula

    They share only the fundamentals of Part I: the explicit link between a zero's real part and the size of its wave.
