Part II · Chapter 7

One slot per mode

A function on the circle of chapter 5 is the same thing as a taken multiples of 1 − γ−s. Such a class is determined by one number for each . This chapter proves the easy half of that statement and checks it numerically.

Based on §1.2.8 of the paper (pages 16–17), read in full. The file RH/Slots.lean proves the statement tested in the lab.

From the half-line to the circle

On the circle of chapter 5, t and γt are the same point. A function ϕ on (0, ∞) gives a function on the circle by summing over all the points that are identified: Φγ(ϕ)(t) = k ϕ(γkt), over all integers k.

Φγ sends some nonzero functions to 0. The simplest is a difference ϕ(t) − ϕ(γt): the sum over k telescopes. By the rescaling property its transform is (1 − γ−s) · M(ϕ)(s), and the factor 1 − γ−s is zero exactly at s = 2πim/ γ, the modes of the circle. The paper states that every function sent to 0 is of this kind:

Φγ(ϕ) = 0 exactly when M(ϕ)(s) is 1 − γ−s times an . So a function on the circle corresponds to a transform modulo multiples of 1 − γ−s. Two transforms agree modulo those multiples exactly when they have the same values at the zeros of 1 − γ−s. A function on the circle is therefore determined by one number for each mode m: one slot per zero of 1 − γ−s.

In Lean

The statement of Lab 7.1 is proved in full (no sorry) against :

theorem mellin_sub_scaled_eq_zero {γ : ℝ} (hγ : 1 < γ) {φ : ℝ → ℂ} (m : ℤ)
    (hφ : MellinConvergent φ (2 * π * Complex.I * m / Real.log γ)) :
    mellin (fun t => φ t - φ (γ * t)) (2 * π * Complex.I * m / Real.log γ) = 0

Things to try

  • In Lab 7.1, sweep γ. The dots move and the zeros of the orange curve move with them: they come from the factor 1 − γ−s, not from the bump.
  • Narrow the bump. Its transform (blue) spreads out to larger heights, and the orange curve spreads with it, but its zeros stay on the dots.

Exercises

  1. Show that Φγ sends ϕ(t) − ϕ(γt) to 0.
    Solution

    Φγ of the difference is Σk ϕ(γkt) − Σk ϕ(γk+1t). The second sum is the first with k shifted by one, and both run over all integers, so they are equal.

  2. For γ = 2, how far apart are the slots on the imaginary axis?
    Solution

    They are at s = 2πim/ln 2, so the spacing is 2π/ln 2 ≈ 9.06. That is the default setting of the lab.

  3. A function ϕ has M(ϕ)(0) = 3. Can Φγ(ϕ) be 0?
    Solution

    No. s = 0 is the slot for m = 0, and a function sent to 0 has a transform that vanishes at every slot. Directly: M(ϕ)(0) = ϕ dt/t, which equals the integral of Φγ(ϕ) once around the circle.
