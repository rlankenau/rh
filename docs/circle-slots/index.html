A function on the circle of chapter 11 is the same thing as a taken multiples of 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s. Such a class is determined by one number for each . This chapter proves the easy half of that statement and checks it numerically.

From the half-line to the circle

On the circle of chapter 11, ttt and γt\gamma tγt are the same point. A function ϕ\phiϕ on (0,∞)(0, \infty)(0,∞) gives a function on the circle by summing over all the points that are identified: Φγ(ϕ)(t)=∑kϕ(γkt)\Phi_{\gamma}(\phi)(t) = \sum_{k} \phi(\gamma^{k}t)Φγ​(ϕ)(t)=∑k​ϕ(γkt), over all integers kkk.

Φγ\Phi_{\gamma}Φγ​ sends some nonzero functions to 0. The simplest is a difference ϕ(t)−ϕ(γt)\phi(t) - \phi(\gamma t)ϕ(t)−ϕ(γt): the sum over kkk telescopes. By the rescaling property its transform is (1−γ−s)⋅M(ϕ)(s)(1 - \gamma^{-s}) \cdot M(\phi)(s)(1−γ−s)⋅M(ϕ)(s), and the factor 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s is zero exactly at s=2πim/ln⁡γs = 2\pi i m/\ln\gammas=2πim/lnγ, the modes of the circle. The paper states that every function sent to 0 is of this kind:

Φγ(ϕ)=0\Phi_{\gamma}(\phi) = 0Φγ​(ϕ)=0 exactly when M(ϕ)(s)M(\phi)(s)M(ϕ)(s) is 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s times an . So a function on the circle corresponds to a transform modulo multiples of 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s. Two transforms agree modulo those multiples exactly when they have the same values at the zeros of 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s. A function on the circle is therefore determined by one number for each mode mmm: one slot per zero of 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s.

Exercises

  1. Show that Φγ\Phi_{\gamma}Φγ​ sends ϕ(t)−ϕ(γt)\phi(t) - \phi(\gamma t)ϕ(t)−ϕ(γt) to 0.
    Solution

    Φγ\Phi_{\gamma}Φγ​ of the difference is ∑kϕ(γkt)−∑kϕ(γk+1t)\sum_{k} \phi(\gamma^{k}t) - \sum_{k} \phi(\gamma^{k+1}t)∑k​ϕ(γkt)−∑k​ϕ(γk+1t). The second sum is the first with kkk shifted by one, and both run over all integers, so they are equal.

  2. For γ=2\gamma = 2γ=2, how far apart are the slots on the imaginary axis?
    Solution

    They are at s=2πimln⁡2s = \dfrac{2\pi i m}{\ln 2}s=ln22πim​, so the spacing is 2πln⁡2≈9.06\dfrac{2\pi}{\ln 2} \approx 9.06ln22π​≈9.06. That is the default setting of the lab.

  3. A function ϕ\phiϕ has M(ϕ)(0)=3M(\phi)(0) = 3M(ϕ)(0)=3. Can Φγ(ϕ)\Phi_{\gamma}(\phi)Φγ​(ϕ) be 0?
    Solution

    No. s=0s = 0s=0 is the slot for m=0m = 0m=0, and a function sent to 0 has a transform that vanishes at every slot. Directly: M(ϕ)(0)=∫ϕ dttM(\phi)(0) = \int \phi\, \frac{dt}{t}M(ϕ)(0)=∫ϕtdt​, which equals the of Φγ(ϕ)\Phi_{\gamma}(\phi)Φγ​(ϕ) once around the circle.

Check your understanding

  1. Two different functions on (0,∞)(0, \infty)(0,∞) give the same function on the circle after summing over powers of γ\gammaγ. What must be true of their Mellin transforms?

    • They are identical
    • They agree at every slot 2πim/ln⁡γ2\pi i m/\ln\gamma2πim/lnγ, and may differ everywhere else
    • They have nothing in common

    Their difference is sent to 0, so its transform is a multiple of 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s and vanishes at the slots. The values at the slots are precisely the Fourier coefficients of the function on the circle.

  2. Polynomials "modulo multiples of x−3x - 3x−3" are determined by one number, the value at 3. What is the corresponding statement in this chapter?

    • Transforms modulo multiples of 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s are determined by their values at its zeros
    • Transforms are determined by their value at s=3s = 3s=3
    • Transforms modulo 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s are all equal

    Adding a multiple of a function cannot change the value where that function is zero, and can change it anywhere else. One slot per zero of 1−γ−s1 - \gamma^{-s}1−γ−s.

  3. Why is the orange curve in the lab zero at every dot, whatever bump you choose?

    • Because the bump is symmetric
    • It is a numerical coincidence
    • Because it is the transform of ϕ(t)−ϕ(γt)\phi(t) - \phi(\gamma t)ϕ(t)−ϕ(γt), which is (1−γ−s)⋅M(ϕ)(s)(1 - \gamma^{-s}) \cdot M(\phi)(s)(1−γ−s)⋅M(ϕ)(s), and the first factor vanishes there

    The zeros belong to the operation "subtract the rescaling", not to the function it is applied to. The same thing happens with ζ\zetaζ in the next chapter.
