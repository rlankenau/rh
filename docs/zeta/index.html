Part I · Chapter 2

The zeta function

The is ζ(s) = 1/ns. This chapter covers its product formula over the , its extension to complex s, and its zeros. The is a statement about where those zeros are.

A sum that is also a product

For real s > 1, the zeta function is the convergent series ζ(s) = 1 + 1/2s + 1/3s + 1/4s + … For example ζ(2) = 1 + 1/4 + 1/9 + 1/16 + … ≈ 1.645. found that the same number is a :

ζ(s) = p prime 1 / (1 − p−s). Multiply the factors out and every whole number appears exactly once, because every whole number factors into primes in exactly one way.

The series runs over all whole numbers; the product runs over primes only. Take the logarithm of the product and differentiate, and the weights of chapter 1 appear: −ζ′(s)/ζ(s) = Σ (n)/ns. The left side blows up wherever ζ(s) = 0. That is how the zeros of ζ enter the counting of primes.

Complex inputs and the critical strip

The series converges only for σ > 1. The function it defines there has a unique to the rest of the , except for at s = 1. Three facts about the extended function organize the subject:

  • Right of 1 there are . There the product over primes is valid, and a product of nonzero factors is not zero.
  • A symmetry. A formula called the ties the value at s to the value at 1 − s. Its effect is that zeros come in mirror pairs on either side of the vertical line σ = ½.
  • What is left. Apart from some easy at −2, −4, −6…, every zero lies in the band between σ = 0 and σ = 1, called the . The line σ = ½ down its middle is the .

The Riemann Hypothesis: every zero in the critical strip is on the critical line. Because zeros come in mirror pairs, this is the same as saying there are none to the right of the line, with σ > ½.

Watching a zero happen

A complex value is zero only when its real and imaginary parts are both zero. Plot ζ(σ + it) in the complex plane as t increases: the point loops around, and a zero is a loop that passes exactly through the origin.

How the lab computes ζ

The defining sum does not settle on a value inside the critical strip, so the lab uses a method due to that does, with 70 terms. Checked against published values it is accurate to about six decimal places for heights up to 60, which is why the labs stop there.

Exercises

  1. Multiply out the Euler product for s = 2 using only the primes 2 and 3, and compare with ζ(2) ≈ 1.645.
    Solution

    1/(1 − ¼) · 1/(1 − 1/9) = (4/3)(9/8) = 1.5. Multiplied out, this is the sum of 1/n² over the numbers n whose only prime factors are 2 and 3. The missing 0.145 comes from every n divisible by a larger prime.

  2. Derive −ζ′(s)/ζ(s) = Σ Λ(n)/ns from the Euler product.
    Solution

    ζ(s) = −Σp ln(1 − p−s). Differentiating, ζ′/ζ = −Σp ln p · p−s/(1 − p−s) = −Σp ln p · (p−s + p−2s + …), by the geometric series. The term p−ks is 1/ns for n = pk, with coefficient ln p = Λ(n).

  3. Suppose ζ had a zero at 0.3 + 14i. Which other zeros would it force?
    Solution

    Zeros come in complex-conjugate pairs, giving 0.3 − 14i. The functional equation pairs s with 1 − s, giving 0.7 − 14i, and its conjugate 0.7 + 14i. So one zero off the line means four, two of them to the right of it.
