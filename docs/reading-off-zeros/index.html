What would have to be checked

  • The divisor and its completion: that the completed has exactly the slots of the zeros inside the strip (chapters 15 and 16).
  • The computation of the in the completed spaces, without the classical (chapter 17).
  • The limit from allowed test functions to φz\varphi_zφz​, uniformly enough in zzz to conclude that the limit is holomorphic.

By chapter 18 the trace formula with the restricted sum is equivalent to the hypothesis, so this last step is not where an error would hide: given the formula, the conclusion follows by the short argument above. The first two items carry the proof. Neither has been verified by anyone as of this writing, and this site has not checked them.

Check your understanding

  1. Why choose the test functions φz(t)=t−z\varphi_z(t) = t^{-z}φz​(t)=t−z?

    • They are the smoothest possible
    • They turn the prime sum into ζ′/ζ\zeta'/\zetaζ′/ζ and each zero's term into a fraction 1z−(ρ−12)\dfrac{1}{z - (\rho - \tfrac12)}z−(ρ−21​)1​, so the formula becomes a statement about poles
    • They vanish at the zeros

    With them, both sides are explicit functions of zzz, and one can compare where each side is allowed to be infinite.

  2. A zero on the critical line also gives a pole of ζ′/ζ\zeta'/\zetaζ′/ζ. Why does it not contradict the conclusion?

    • Its pole is at Re⁡z=0\operatorname{Re} z = 0Rez=0, outside the half-plane Re⁡z>a\operatorname{Re} z \gt aRez>a the argument is about
    • Zeros on the line give no poles
    • Its pole is canceled by G(z)G(z)G(z)

    The argument says: no poles with Re⁡z>a\operatorname{Re} z \gt aRez>a except z=12z = \tfrac12z=21​. On-line zeros sit on the imaginary axis of the zzz-plane, to the left of that region, and they do appear in the sum on the right.

  3. Why must the formula hold for arbitrarily small aaa, not just for one value?

    • For a smaller aaa the formula is easier to prove
    • It need not
    • Each aaa only excludes zeros with Re⁡ρ>12+a\operatorname{Re}\rho \gt \tfrac12 + aReρ>21​+a; zeros closer to the line are excluded only by letting a→0a \to 0a→0

    One fixed aaa gives a zero-free region, not the hypothesis. The claim that the construction works for every a>0a \gt 0a>0 is essential.
