What a zero off the line would do

If every zero is on the , β=12\beta = \tfrac12β=21​, then every wave has amplitude about x\sqrt{x}x​, and the total gap is about x\sqrt{x}x​: the bound from chapter 1. A single zero with β\betaβ bigger than 12\tfrac1221​ would contribute a wave of size xβx^{\beta}xβ, which eventually drowns out all the others. This is why the and the square-root bound on the gap are the same statement.

The mirror symmetry of chapter 8 matters here. A zero to the left of the line has a partner the same distance to the right, so any zero off the line produces a wave that grows faster than x\sqrt{x}x​.
