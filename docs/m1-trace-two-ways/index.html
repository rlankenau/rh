How the example corresponds to the paper

In the paper the circle is replaced by a much larger group, built from all the of a , and the operator is again convolution with a test function φ\varphiφ. The two sides of the trace become:

  • spectral side: one term M(φ)(ρ−12)M(\varphi)(\rho - \tfrac12)M(φ)(ρ−21​) for each zero ρ\rhoρ of the L-function, in place of one term for each mode;
  • geometric side: each prime ppp contributes like a circle of circumference ln⁡p\ln plnp, giving terms ln⁡p⋅φ(pk)\ln p \cdot \varphi(p^{k})lnp⋅φ(pk) at the .

The resulting identity, a sum over zeros equal to a sum over prime powers for every test function φ\varphiφ, is a form of the of chapter 9.

When the trace is zero

If φ\varphiφ is zero outside an interval strictly between 1 and γ\gammaγ, then φ(γk)=0\varphi(\gamma^{k}) = 0φ(γk)=0 for every integer kkk. The geometric side is 0, so the sum of the eigenvalues is 0, although the individual eigenvalues are not. The paper uses this to remove all but finitely many primes: for a fixed φ\varphiφ, every prime ppp larger than the right end of the interval where φ\varphiφ is nonzero contributes 0.

Check your understanding

  1. For φ≥0\varphi \ge 0φ≥0 supported inside (1,γ)(1, \gamma)(1,γ) the trace is 0, yet the eigenvalue of the constant mode, M(φ)(0)=∫φ dttM(\varphi)(0) = \int \varphi\, \frac{dt}{t}M(φ)(0)=∫φtdt​, is positive. How can both be true?

    • The other eigenvalues are complex numbers with varying phases, and together they cancel it exactly
    • The other eigenvalues are all 0
    • The trace formula fails for such φ\varphiφ

    The trace is the sum of all eigenvalues, not a statement about each one. That an infinite sum of nonzero terms must total exactly 0 is real information about where the modes are.

  2. Where does the term φ(γk)\varphi(\gamma^{k})φ(γk) on the geometric side come from?

    • From the kkk-th eigenvalue
    • On the circle, xxx and γkx\gamma^{k}xγkx are the same point, so the kernel on the diagonal collects φ\varphiφ once for each way of going round kkk times
    • From the kkk-th derivative of φ\varphiφ

    K(x,x)=∑kφ(γk)K(x, x) = \sum_{k} \varphi(\gamma^{k})K(x,x)=∑k​φ(γk): the operator moves a point by the factor ttt with weight φ(t)\varphi(t)φ(t), and it returns to where it started exactly when ttt is a power of γ\gammaγ.
