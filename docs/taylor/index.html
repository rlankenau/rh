Taylor series

A Taylor series rebuilds a function from its derivatives at a single point. This chapter recalls how the polynomials are formed, what each term contributes, and what it means for the series to converge.

Approximating a function by polynomials

Near a point ccc, called the center, a function that has derivatives of every order can be approximated by polynomials built from its derivatives at ccc:

Three series to know

All three are about the center c=0c = 0c=0:

Three things a Taylor series can do

  • Converge to the function everywhere. exe^{x}ex and sin⁡x\sin xsinx.
  • Converge to the function on an interval and diverge outside it. 1/(1−x)1/(1-x)1/(1−x), ln⁡(1+x)\ln(1+x)ln(1+x) and 1/(1+x2)1/(1+x^{2})1/(1+x2), each with R=1R = 1R=1 about the center 0.
  • Converge, but to the wrong function. Let g(x)=0g(x) = 0g(x)=0 for x≤0x \le 0x≤0 and g(x)=e−1/xg(x) = e^{-1/x}g(x)=e−1/x for x>0x \gt 0x>0. So "has a Taylor series" and "equals its Taylor series" are different properties. Chapter 5 is about the functions that have the second one.
