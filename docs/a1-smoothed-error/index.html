The smoothed error

The Alpöge does not bound ψ(x)−x\psi(x) - xψ(x)−x. It bounds Gz(x)\mathrm{Gz}(x)Gz(x), the error term of a weighted of ψ\psiψ. Integrating divides the term for each zero by one more factor of size ∣ρ∣|\rho|∣ρ∣, and that makes the sum over zeros converge absolutely.

Based on the definitions of Sz, Gz and ZetaLandau in the source, which I read as statements. The curves are computed here from those definitions and from the first 30 zeros; the match between the two is a check I ran, not part of the formalization.

Why one side is enough

The formalization proves an upper bound on Gz and no lower bound. A makes that sufficient. The Lean statement ZetaLandau covers either sign: if s0⋅Gz(x)≤C⋅xθs_{0}\cdot \mathrm{Gz}(x) \le C\cdot x^{\theta}s0​⋅Gz(x)≤C⋅xθ for all large xxx, with s0=+1s_{0} = +1s0​=+1 or −1-1−1, then ζ\zetaζ has no zeros with greater than θ\thetaθ.

A zero with real part β>θ\beta \gt \thetaβ>θ contributes an oscillating term of amplitude proportional to xβx^{\beta}xβ, which takes both signs. On its own that term exceeds C⋅xθC\cdot x^{\theta}C⋅xθ in both directions for large xxx. 's theorem shows that the terms from the other zeros cannot cancel it.

Check your understanding

  1. What is gained by bounding the smoothed error Gz and not ψ(x)−x\psi(x) - xψ(x)−x directly?

    • Gz has no contribution from zeros
    • The amplitudes become about 2/γ22/\gamma^{2}2/γ2, whose sum over all zeros is finite, so the sum over zeros converges absolutely
    • Gz is easier to compute by hand

    For ψ\psiψ the amplitudes are about 2/γ2/\gamma2/γ, and their sum diverges; the explicit formula then converges only through cancellation. The smoothed version can be bounded term by term.

  2. Why is Sz continuous although ψ\psiψ jumps at every prime power?

    • Because the weight x/n−1x/n - 1x/n−1 is 0 at the moment nnn enters the sum
    • Because primes are ignored
    • Because it is rounded

    A new term appears at x=nx = nx=n with weight 0 and then grows linearly. That gives a corner, not a jump. Integrating a function with jumps always does this.

  3. In Lab 18.2 a hypothetical zero with β=0.55\beta = 0.55β=0.55 stays inside the band up to 20,000. What does that say about testing the hypothesis by computing with primes?

    • It confirms the hypothesis
    • That such a zero is impossible
    • A zero close to the line would be invisible in any range of xxx one can compute, so such tests cannot settle it

    The excess factor is x0.05x^{0.05}x0.05, only 1.6 at 20,000, and a zero at great height has a tiny amplitude as well. Evidence from primes in a finite range is not a proof.
