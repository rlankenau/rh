Quiz: Part I

Eight questions on chapters 1 to 3.

Choose an answer to see whether it is right and why. Your best score is kept in this browser only.

  1. What does Chebyshev's function ψ(x) add up?

    • 1 for each prime up to x
    • ln p for each prime power pk up to x
    • 1/n for each whole number up to x
    • ln n for each whole number up to x

    ψ(x) = Σn ≤ x Λ(n), and Λ(n) = ln p when n is a power of the prime p and 0 otherwise. Counting 1 per prime gives π(x) instead.

  2. ψ(10) equals:

    • ln 2520 ≈ 7.83
    • ln 210 ≈ 5.35
    • 4
    • 10

    The prime powers up to 10 are 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, giving 3 ln 2 + 2 ln 3 + ln 5 + ln 7 = ln 2520. ln 210 counts each prime once and forgets 4, 8 and 9; 4 is π(10).

  3. Why is the sum Σ 1/ns equal to a product with one factor per prime?

    • Because there are infinitely many primes
    • Because the series converges for s > 1
    • Because every whole number factors into primes in exactly one way
    • Because of the functional equation

    Multiplying out the factors 1 + p−s + p−2s + … produces each 1/ns exactly once, which is unique factorization. Convergence is needed for the manipulation but is not the reason.

  4. The Riemann Hypothesis says that every zero of ζ in the critical strip has:

    • imaginary part ½
    • real part ½
    • real part between 0 and 1
    • absolute value ½

    The critical line is Re s = ½. "Real part between 0 and 1" is the critical strip itself, which is a theorem, not a hypothesis.

  5. The functional equation pairs the value of ζ at s with its value at:

    • −s
    • 1/s
    • s + 1
    • 1 − s

    So zeros are mirror images across the line Re s = ½: a zero at 0.3 + 14i would come with one at 0.7 − 14i, and with both conjugates.

  6. In the explicit formula, a zero ρ = β + iγ contributes a wave to ψ(x) − x. Its amplitude grows like:

    • xγ
    • ln x
    • xβ
    • √γ

    |xρ| = xβ. The height γ sets the frequency, cos(γ ln x), and the real part β sets the size. Under the hypothesis every wave has size √x.

  7. Where does −ζ′(s)/ζ(s) = Σ Λ(n)/ns become infinite?

    • Only at s = 1
    • At the zeros of ζ and at its pole
    • Nowhere
    • At every prime

    Dividing by ζ gives a pole wherever ζ = 0, and the pole of ζ at s = 1 gives one too. This is how zeros of ζ enter statements about primes.

  8. The wave of the first zero (γ ≈ 14.13) repeats each time x is multiplied by about:

    • 1.56
    • 14.13
    • 2
    • e ≈ 2.72

    cos(γ ln x) repeats when ln x grows by 2π/γ ≈ 0.445, and e0.445 ≈ 1.56. The waves are periodic in ln x, not in x.
